### Task Design Factors

range of properties to challenge RL algorithms:

- Narrow and biased data distributions
  - arise in human demonstrations
  - arise with hand crafted policies
  - important in ORL: the ability to handle diverse distributions while not producing worse than provided behaviour.

- Undirected and multitask learning:
  - when data is passively logged, the task of the agent may not be performed in the collected data but must be stitched together from multiple observations (e.g. videotapes of a car driving from A to B and B to C exists and the ride from A to C must be inferred)

- Sparse rewards
  - difficulty of credit assignment and exploration
  - algorithms must have the ability to perform credit assignment

- Suboptimal data
  - data may not show optimal behavior, i.e. just imitating the data does not lead to optimal results
  - e.g. imitation learning does not make sense on non expert (non perfect) demonstrations

- Non representable behavior policies, non Markovian behavior policies and partial observalibilty:
  - TLDR; data from the real world may be created by a process that cannot be modeled as the model lacks capabilities (e.g. full observation of the state)

markovian: next state solely depends on state and taken action

non-markovian: next state depends on state, action and history (you may visit a bank multiple times in your life, put pulling a gun and shouting "this is a robbery" surely will not lead to success every time)

- Realistic domains:
  - the real world would be ideal for benchmarks, but sampling here is rather costly, especially for our bank robber agent
